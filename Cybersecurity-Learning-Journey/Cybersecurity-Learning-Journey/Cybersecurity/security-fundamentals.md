# Security Fundamentals

## Authentication

Authentication is the process of checking that a person or system is who or what it claims to be.

Examples include passwords, security keys, and biometric checks.

## Authorization

Authorization determines what an authenticated user or system is allowed to access or do.

Authentication answers “Who are you?” Authorization answers “What are you allowed to do?”

## Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA requires two or more different types of verification. Common factor categories include:

- Something you know, such as a password.
- Something you have, such as a security key.
- Something you are, such as a biometric characteristic.

MFA can reduce the risk of account compromise, although it does not remove every risk.

## Encryption

Encryption transforms readable data into a form that cannot normally be understood without the appropriate key. It helps protect the confidentiality of information.

## Hashing

Hashing uses an algorithm to produce a fixed-length digest from input data. Cryptographic hashes are designed to make it difficult to recover the original input or find two different inputs with the same digest.

Hashing and encryption are not the same: hashing is generally one-way, while encryption is designed to be reversible with the correct key.

## Firewall

A firewall applies rules to network traffic. Depending on its type and configuration, it can allow or block traffic based on details such as addresses, ports, protocols, or application information.

## Antivirus and endpoint protection

Antivirus and endpoint protection tools help identify, block, or investigate suspicious activity on devices. Their capabilities vary, and they should be part of a wider security approach.

## Vulnerability, threat, and risk

- **Vulnerability:** a weakness that could be used or triggered.
- **Threat:** something capable of causing harm.
- **Risk:** the possibility and impact of harm occurring in a particular context.

## Least privilege

Least privilege means giving a user or process only the access needed to perform its task, and no more.
