# Common Cybersecurity Threats

This document introduces several common threat types from a defensive and awareness perspective.

## Phishing

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker uses a misleading message or website to trick someone into revealing information, opening an unsafe attachment, or taking another unwanted action.

**Possible warning signs:**
- Unexpected requests for passwords or payment.
- A sender address that does not match the claimed organisation.
- Unusual urgency or pressure.
- Links or attachments that were not expected.

**Defensive measures:** Verify requests through a trusted channel, use multi-factor authentication, report suspicious messages, and provide user awareness training.

## Malware

Malware is software designed to perform unwanted or harmful actions, such as stealing information, disrupting systems, or providing unauthorised access.

**Defensive measures:** Keep software updated, use reputable endpoint protection, restrict unnecessary privileges, and maintain tested backups.

## Ransomware

Ransomware is a type of malware that can make data or systems unavailable, often by encrypting files, and may be used to demand payment.

**Defensive measures:** Maintain offline or otherwise protected backups, patch systems, restrict access, use endpoint protection, and prepare an incident-response plan.

## Social engineering

Social engineering uses deception or manipulation to influence people into revealing information or performing actions that weaken security.

**Defensive measures:** Verify unusual requests, follow approval procedures, and avoid sharing credentials.

## Brute-force attempts

A brute-force attempt involves repeated guesses against an authentication mechanism.

**Defensive measures:** Use strong unique passwords, multi-factor authentication, rate limiting, account lockout policies where appropriate, and monitoring for unusual login patterns.

## Denial of Service (DoS)

A denial-of-service attack attempts to make a service unavailable to legitimate users by exhausting resources or disrupting normal operation.

**Defensive measures:** Use suitable network protections, monitoring, capacity planning, and an incident-response process.

## Blue Team perspective

A defensive analyst looks for evidence, considers the context, records findings, and follows the organisation's response process. A single warning sign should not automatically be treated as proof of an incident.
